Questions

Are two days a week enough?

Two days a week doesn’t mean two days in a row. The hours can be split, for example into four half days, so outreach, meetings and follow-ups run across the whole week, not just on two of its days. Two days of the right work beat five days of guessing, and you don’t pay for idle days.

What happens when you leave?

That’s the point. Everything we build, the strategy, the playbooks, the team, is designed to run without us. A market that depends on one person isn’t a market. It’s a hire.

How many market entries have you led?

I haven’t run forty market entries from a boardroom. I’ve built DACH from the front line: I helped open a German market for an FX platform, I’ve sold into DACH my entire career, and I hit quota in 6 months at a European fintech’s German office, where most reps need around 18. You’re not buying slides from someone who used to sell. You’re buying someone who sells now.

How many meetings or deals can you guarantee?

None, and anyone who guarantees a number in a market they haven’t tested yet is guessing. What we commit to: within the first three months, we book and run the first meetings with your prospects, and you’ll know whether the problem is sales or product.

Why not just hire a country manager?

You should, once you know what to judge them on. We define the role with you, assess candidates in German, and train the person you hire on a playbook that already works in DACH. Then they take over.

Who is this not for?

Companies that want to buy leads or appointments instead of a strategy, expect results in 30 days, or want to hand DACH off for good without building their own team. And anyone who would rather hear confirmation than an honest read.

All three. Most companies start with Germany, Europe’s largest economy, and build Austria and Switzerland on the same foundation.
