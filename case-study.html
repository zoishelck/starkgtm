Case study

One champion.Ten departments.15,000 users.

How we turned a single enthusiastic contact into €25M in annual spend in 12 months

The challenge

Every enterprise deal starts the same way: someone inside the company loves your product. Our client had that. One champion got excited and brought in a senior sponsor, who got excited too.

But excitement doesn't get a contract signed. This deal needed finance, legal, tax, management and finally the board to say yes. And signing was only half of it. Our client earns money when customers actually run spend through their platform, so a signed contract that nobody uses would have been worth nothing.

How we did it

  1. 01

    We didn't let the champion do our selling.

    Champions are great at opening doors, but they aren't salespeople. If you leave them to pitch your product internally, the message gets lost somewhere between departments. So we used her enthusiasm to get introductions, and we pitched every stakeholder ourselves, early. That came to 10 decision-makers and around 20 meetings, all the way up to the board.

  2. 02

    We kept the deal alive when it stalled.

    Halfway through, the board's priorities changed and our champion was pulled onto another project. The deal was put on hold. Because we had already built relationships across the organization, other people stepped up to push it forward, and six months after the first meeting, the board signed.

  3. 03

    We treated implementation as a second sale.

    A signature isn't revenue; usage is. We went back to every department, learned how they actually work, and adapted the system to fit their processes, not the other way around. That took six months and 25–30 meetings.

  4. 04

    We trained 15,000 people with one session.

    We ran a live training with 10 representatives, one from each user group, and recorded it. The client made watching it mandatory company-wide.

The result

  • 15,000active users
  • €25Min annual spend through the platform, up from zero
  • 12 monthsfrom first meeting to go-live

What this means for you

If you're bringing your product into a new market, your first champion is the easy part. The hard part is everyone they have to convince, and everyone who has to actually use it afterwards. That's the part we take on.
